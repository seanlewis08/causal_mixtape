Session 7 · Panel data and the 2×2 difference-in-differences

Book chapter 8 and sections 9.1–9.3

Read before the meeting: Chapter 8 — Panel Data and Chapter 9, sections 9.1–9.3. Data: data/sasp_panel.dta; John Snow’s cholera table (typed in). Code files: code/session07.py · code/session07.R · Slides

Learning goals

Participants should be able to write the fixed-effects model and explain what within variation is, implement FE three ways (dummies, demeaning, a panel routine) and show they agree, say what FE can and cannot fix (time-invariant versus time-varying unobservables), and compute a 2×2 difference-in-differences by hand, as a regression, and in terms of the parallel-trends assumption.

Key ideas

The fixed-effects model. \(Y_{it} = \delta D_{it} + \mathbf{X}_{it}\beta + u_i + \varepsilon_{it}\). The unit effect \(u_i\) can be correlated with \(D_{it}\) in any way; FE removes it by comparing each unit to itself across time. Three equivalent implementations: unit dummies, demeaning every variable within unit, or first-differencing (with two periods).

What FE needs. Strict exogeneity: \(E[\varepsilon_{it} \mid D_{i1}, \dots, D_{iT}, u_i] = 0\). Time-varying unobservables that move with \(D\) are still a problem. Reverse causality and feedback (today’s shock affects tomorrow’s treatment) violate it. FE also needs within variation; a regressor that never changes for a unit is absorbed.

The SASP question. Sex workers charge more for unprotected sex. Is that a compensating differential for risk, or do higher-priced providers simply differ in ways that also make them more likely to offer it? The panel follows the same provider across clients, so provider-level unobservables drop out.

The 2×2 DiD. Two groups, two periods. \(\hat{\delta} = (\bar{Y}_{T,post} - \bar{Y}_{T,pre}) - (\bar{Y}_{C,post} - \bar{Y}_{C,pre})\). The identifying assumption is parallel trends: absent treatment, the treated group’s outcome would have moved by the same amount as the control group’s. It is a statement about a counterfactual and is untestable in the 2×2 case. As a regression: \(Y = \alpha + \beta\,\text{Treat} + \gamma\,\text{Post} + \delta\,(\text{Treat} \times \text{Post}) + e\).

John Snow. In 1854 London, the Lambeth water company had moved its intake upstream of the sewage; Southwark & Vauxhall had not. Cholera deaths fell in Lambeth-served households and rose elsewhere. It is the first DiD and still one of the cleanest.

Walkthrough

Step 1 — SASP: pooled OLS

  • R
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import statsmodels.formula.api as smf
from linearmodels.panel import PanelOLS

sasp = pd.read_stata("data/sasp_panel.dta", convert_categoricals=False)

timevar = ["unsafe", "llength", "reg", "asq_cl", "appearance_cl", "provider_second",
           "asian_cl", "black_cl", "hispanic_cl", "othrace_cl", "hot", "massage_cl", "age_cl"]
fixed   = ["age", "asq", "bmi", "hispanic", "black", "other", "asian", "schooling",
           "cohab", "married", "divorced", "separated"]
sasp = sasp.dropna(subset=["lnw", "id"] + timevar + fixed)

pooled = smf.ols("lnw ~ " + " + ".join(timevar + fixed), data=sasp)\
            .fit(cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": sasp["id"]})
print("pooled OLS, unsafe:", pooled.params["unsafe"].round(3), " SE", pooled.bse["unsafe"].round(3))
library(tidyverse); library(haven); library(fixest)

unsafe ≈ 0.00 (SE 0.03): pooled across providers, there is no price premium for unprotected sex once client and provider characteristics are controlled. About 1,500 sessions enter after dropping missing values.

Step 2 — Fixed effects, three ways

  • Python
  • R
# (a) demeaning by hand
dm = sasp.copy()
for v in ["lnw"] + timevar:
    dm[v] = sasp[v] - sasp.groupby("id")[v].transform("mean")
fe_dm = smf.ols("lnw ~ " + " + ".join(timevar), data=dm)\
           .fit(cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": dm["id"]})

# (b) unit dummies (slow with many ids, but transparent)
fe_dummy = smf.ols("lnw ~ " + " + ".join(timevar) + " + C(id)", data=sasp).fit()

# (c) PanelOLS
p = sasp.set_index(["id", "session"])
fe_panel = PanelOLS.from_formula("lnw ~ " + " + ".join(timevar) + " + EntityEffects", data=p)\
             .fit(cov_type="clustered", cluster_entity=True)

print("demeaned:", fe_dm.params["unsafe"].round(3),
      " dummies:", fe_dummy.params["unsafe"].round(3),
      " PanelOLS:", fe_panel.params["unsafe"].round(3), " SE", fe_panel.std_errors["unsafe"].round(3))
f_fe <- as.formula(paste("lnw ~", paste(timevar, collapse = " + "), "| id"))
fe <- feols(f_fe, data = sasp, cluster = ~id)
coeftable(fe)["unsafe", ]

# the same thing by hand
dm <- sasp %>% group_by(id) %>%
  mutate(across(all_of(c("lnw", timevar)), ~ .x - mean(.x, na.rm = TRUE))) %>% ungroup()
coef(lm(as.formula(paste("lnw ~", paste(timevar, collapse = " + "))), data = dm))["unsafe"]

etable(pooled, fe, keep = "unsafe")

All three give unsafe ≈ 0.05 (SE ≈ 0.024): within the same provider, an unprotected session commands about a 5% premium. The time-invariant provider characteristics (age, schooling, race, marital status) are absorbed; try adding them to the FE model and watch them vanish or error. The dummy version’s SE is wrong (not clustered) — note it and move on.

Step 3 — Why they differ

The pooled estimate mixes two comparisons: within a provider (does she charge more when the session is unprotected?) and between providers (do the ones who offer unprotected sex charge more in general?). FE keeps only the first. The near-zero pooled estimate means the between-provider correlation is negative enough to cancel the within premium: providers willing to offer unprotected sex are, on average, lower-priced. That is exactly the selection story FE was built to remove.

Lambeth (treated: moved intake upstream in 1852) 85 19

snow = pd.DataFrame({
    "company": ["SV", "SV", "Lambeth", "Lambeth"],
    "year":    [1849, 1854, 1849, 1854],
    "deaths":  [135, 147, 85, 19],
})

by_hand = (19 - 85) - (147 - 135)
reg = smf.ols("deaths ~ treat * post", data=snow).fit()
print("by hand:", by_hand, "  regression:", reg.params["treat:post"].round(1))
snow <- tibble(company = c("SV","SV","Lambeth","Lambeth"),
               year = c(1849, 1854, 1849, 1854),
               deaths = c(135, 147, 85, 19)) %>%
  mutate(treat = as.integer(company == "Lambeth"), post = as.integer(year == 1854))

(19 - 85) - (147 - 135)
coef(lm(deaths ~ treat * post, data = snow))["treat:post"]

Both give −78 deaths per 10,000 households. With four cells there are no standard errors — the regression is exactly identified — which is the point: the 2×2 is an identification argument, and inference needs more groups or more periods (next session).

Step 5 — Draw the counterfactual

Sketch (or plot) the four points. The DiD estimate is the gap between Lambeth’s actual 1854 value (19) and what parallel trends says it would have been: \(85 + (147 - 135) = 97\). Ask the room what would make the dotted line wrong — for example, if Lambeth’s neighborhoods were already becoming cleaner for other reasons.

Exercises

  1. First differences vs FE. Keep only providers with exactly two sessions in the SASP data (about 68 of them). Estimate the unsafe effect by first-differencing (session 2 minus session 1, regression without a constant) and by FE on the demeaned data. Show they are identical with \(T = 2\), explain what adding a constant to the FD regression corresponds to in the FE model, and say in one sentence why FD and FE diverge for \(T > 2\).
  2. What FE cannot fix. Simulate a panel of 500 units × 5 periods where \(D_{it}\) responds to last period’s shock: \(D_{it} = 1[\varepsilon_{i,t-1} > 0]\). Set the true effect to 1. Show that FE is biased and explain which assumption failed.
  3. Snow with a placebo. Suppose a third company, “Chelsea,” had deaths of 110 in 1849 and 125 in 1854 and did not move its intake. Compute the DiD of Chelsea vs Southwark & Vauxhall. What does a near-zero number tell you about parallel trends, and what does it not tell you?

Solutions

Solutions (click to expand)

1. With \(T = 2\), demeaning and differencing use the same single within-unit contrast, so the estimates coincide exactly (≈ −0.06 in this small subsample — note it has the opposite sign from the full-panel FE estimate, a reminder that 68 providers is not much data). Adding a constant to the FD regression is the same as adding a period fixed effect to the FE model. For \(T > 2\) FE uses all pairwise contrasts weighted by the within-unit variance, while FD uses only adjacent-period contrasts; they differ, and FD is more robust when \(\varepsilon_{it}\) is a random walk.

2. FE is biased because strict exogeneity fails: \(D_{it}\) depends on \(\varepsilon_{i,t-1}\), and demeaning puts every period’s \(\varepsilon\) into every period’s transformed error. The bias shrinks as \(T\) grows (it is of order \(1/T\)). First differencing also fails here, because \(\Delta D_{it}\) depends on \(\varepsilon_{i,t-1}\) which is in \(\Delta \varepsilon_{it}\).

rng = np.random.default_rng(0); N, T = 500, 5
e = rng.normal(size=(N, T)); D = np.zeros((N, T), int); D[:, 1:] = (e[:, :-1] > 0)
u = rng.normal(size=(N, 1)); Y = 1 * D + u + e
df = pd.DataFrame({"y": Y.ravel(), "d": D.ravel(), "i": np.repeat(np.arange(N), T), "t": np.tile(np.arange(T), N)})
dm = df.assign(y=df.y - df.groupby("i").y.transform("mean"), d=df.d - df.groupby("i").d.transform("mean"))
print(smf.ols("y ~ d", dm).fit().params["d"])    # noticeably below 1

3. Chelsea vs S&V: \((125 - 110) - (147 - 135) = 3\): two untreated companies moved in parallel. That is evidence consistent with parallel trends for the untreated, which makes the assumption more plausible for Lambeth. It does not test Lambeth’s own counterfactual trend, which is unobservable by construction.

Facilitator notes

Facilitator notes (click to expand)

Step 2 is the demo. Three implementations agreeing to three decimals is what makes “fixed effects” stop being a black box. Have one person run the demeaned version and another the panel routine, and compare live.

Misconception: FE controls for everything about the unit. Everything fixed about the unit. Say “time-invariant” every time you say “fixed effect” for the first ten minutes.

Misconception: the pooled and FE estimates disagreeing means one is wrong. They answer different questions (Step 3). Only one answers the causal question, and only under strict exogeneity.

On the data. The SASP file loads provider_second as a categorical in pandas; convert_categoricals=False avoids a crash in the demeaning step. In R, haven keeps it numeric.

If running short. Compress Steps 4–5 to the by-hand calculation and the sketch; the regression version recurs next session.

Discussion prompts. (a) In SASP, what time-varying unobservable could still bias the FE estimate? (b) Why is the 2×2 DiD a special case of FE (two units, two periods)?

Bridge to next session. The 2×2 has no SEs and no test of parallel trends. With many groups and many periods you get both — and a new problem when treatment turns on at different times.

Directed acyclic graphs

Session 2 · Mixtape chapter 3

Last time

  • Regression anatomy: “controlling for” is mechanical
  • The open question: which variables should you control for?
  • Regression cannot answer that. A DAG can.

DAG grammar

  • Nodes are variables; \(A \to B\) means \(A\) causes \(B\)
  • A missing arrow is a strong claim: no direct effect
  • The graph encodes what you believe before looking at data
  • Acyclic: no variable causes itself

Paths

  • A path is any sequence of edges between \(D\) and \(Y\), ignoring direction
  • Backdoor path: starts with an arrow into \(D\) — carries non-causal association
  • A path is blocked if it contains
    • a non-collider you condition on, or
    • a collider you do not condition on

Confounder vs collider

Confounder — open by default

Control for \(X\) → path closes

Collider — closed by default

Control for \(C\) → path opens

The backdoor criterion

\(X\) identifies the effect of \(D\) on \(Y\) if

  1. \(X\) blocks every backdoor path from \(D\) to \(Y\), and
  2. \(X\) contains no descendant of \(D\)
  • The set can be empty (randomization)
  • Everything in Sessions 4 and 7 is a bet that some \(X\) satisfies this

The discrimination DAG

“Control for occupation” opens discrimination → occupation ← ability → wage. High-ability women who made it into a given occupation despite discrimination are paid for their ability, so within occupations discrimination looks harmless.

Selection is conditioning too

Beauty and talent are unrelated in the population. Among stars — people selected on the sum of the two — they are strongly negatively related: a star who is not very talented must be very beautiful. Looking only at stars is conditioning on the collider.

Takeaway

More controls are not safer. The right adjustment set comes from the graph, and the graph comes from theory and institutional knowledge — not from the data.
